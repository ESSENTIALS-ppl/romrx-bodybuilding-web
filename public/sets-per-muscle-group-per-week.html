How Many Sets Per Muscle Group Per Week for Hypertrophy?

If you want to build muscle, weekly sets matter more than almost anything else. This is your total number of hard, challenging sets for one muscle across a full week. Get this number right and growth follows. This guide gives you the science-backed range and how to use it.

What Does the Research Say About Weekly Sets?

More sets usually means more muscle, up to a point. A large review by Brad Schoenfeld and colleagues found that muscle growth rises as weekly sets rise. Groups doing 10 or more sets per week grew more than groups doing fewer (Schoenfeld et al., 2017, Journal of Sports Sciences).

Each extra weekly set was linked to about a 0.37% gain in muscle size in the pooled data, and 9 or more sets beat fewer than 9 (House of Hypertrophy summary).

The benefit is not endless, though. More sets help until your body can no longer recover from them. After that, extra volume just adds fatigue (Scinergy review of the Schoenfeld data).

The Practical Weekly Set Range

Use your training age to pick a starting range. These are hard working sets, not warm-ups.

Training levelWeekly sets per muscleNotes
Beginner8–12Grows well on lower volume; focus on form first
Intermediate12–18The sweet spot for most lifters
Advanced18–22+Only if recovery, sleep, and nutrition are dialed in

Start at the low end of your range. Add 1 to 2 sets per muscle every couple of weeks, but only if you are still recovering and still getting stronger.

Why Frequency Changes the Math

You do not need to cram all your sets into one day. Splitting weekly sets across two or more sessions lets each set be higher quality. Training a muscle at least twice per week beats once per week when total sets are equal (Verro Training review of frequency research).

So 16 weekly sets for back might become 8 sets on Monday and 8 sets on Thursday. Same volume, fresher effort, better results.

What Counts as One Hard Set?

A set only counts toward your weekly total if it is close to failure and uses a full range of motion. Aim to stop within one to three reps of failure on most sets.

Range of motion matters here too. Training a muscle through its full length, especially the deep, stretched position, produces equal or greater growth than short, partial reps (The Strength Equation on stretch-mediated hypertrophy). Ten deep, controlled sets can beat sixteen shallow ones.

Key Takeaways

  • Most lifters grow best on 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle per week.
  • Beginners need less; advanced lifters can handle more, if recovery allows.
  • Split your sets across 2+ sessions per week for higher-quality effort.
  • Only count sets taken near failure through a full range of motion.
  • Add volume slowly, and only while you are still recovering and progressing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 10 sets per week enough to build muscle?

Yes. For most lifters, 10 to 12 hard weekly sets per muscle drives solid growth, especially for beginners and intermediates.

Can too many sets stop muscle growth?

Yes. Past your recovery limit, extra sets add fatigue without extra muscle.

Should I split my sets across the week?

Yes. Training a muscle two or more times per week beats once per week when total volume is equal.

What counts as one hard set?

A set taken close to failure, usually within one to three reps of failure, through a full range of motion. Easy warm-up sets do not count toward your weekly total.
