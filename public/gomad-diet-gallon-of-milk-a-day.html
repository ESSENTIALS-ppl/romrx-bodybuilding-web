GOMAD Diet: Does a Gallon of Milk a Day Actually Work?

GOMAD stands for Gallon Of Milk A Day. It is a classic hardgainer trick for putting on weight fast. But does it actually build muscle, or just fat? Here is the honest breakdown.

What Is in a Gallon of Milk?

A gallon of whole milk is a big load of calories and protein on top of your normal food.

NutrientPer gallon of whole milk
CaloriesAbout 2,400
ProteinAbout 123 to 128 g
CarbsAbout 187 g
FatAbout 127 g

Those numbers come from multiple nutrition breakdowns of whole milk (Transparent Labs GOMAD breakdown).

Does It Build Muscle?

GOMAD will make you gain weight, and paired with lifting, some of it will be muscle. But a large share will be fat, because the surplus is far bigger than you need. Your body cannot use that many extra calories at once, so the leftovers get stored as fat (Healthline on GOMAD).

Remember, the research-supported surplus for lean gains is only about 250 to 500 calories per day (Sports Medicine - Open, 2023). GOMAD adds roughly 2,400, so it overshoots by a lot.

The Downsides

  • Lots of the weight gained is fat, not muscle.
  • High saturated fat and sodium in a gallon of whole milk.
  • Bloating, stomach upset, and acne are common.
  • Gains often reverse once you stop (Bon Appetit on GOMAD).

Who Might Consider It

GOMAD is really only aimed at true hardgainers who genuinely cannot eat enough to gain weight. Even then, most coaches suggest starting with half a gallon and letting the scale guide the amount (FuelTheGains on whole milk for bulking).

A Smarter Alternative

Instead of a full gallon, add a controlled 250 to 500 calorie surplus using milk-based shakes folded into real meals. You get the easy calories and protein without the bloat, the fat gain, and the blood-work concerns. Let the scale drive the amount: if you gain faster than about 0.5 kg per week, cut back.

Key Takeaways

  • A gallon of whole milk adds about 2,400 calories and 128 g protein.
  • It builds some muscle, but a lot of the gain is fat.
  • The surplus is far bigger than the 250 to 500 calories you actually need.
  • Half a gallon or milk-based shakes in real meals is the smarter play.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the GOMAD diet build muscle?

It builds some muscle when paired with lifting, but a large share of the weight gained is fat because the surplus is far too big.

How many calories are in a gallon of milk?

About 2,400 calories, with roughly 128 g of protein, in a gallon of whole milk.

Is GOMAD healthy?

Not for most people. It is high in saturated fat and sodium and often causes bloating and acne.

What should I do instead of GOMAD?

Use a small 250 to 500 calorie surplus, adding milk-based shakes to real meals, and let the scale guide the amount.
