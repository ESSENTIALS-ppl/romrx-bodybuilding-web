Best Supplements for Muscle Growth: What Actually Works

The supplement aisle is huge, but the list that actually works is short. Most muscle-building supplements do very little. Here are the few backed by strong research, and the ones to skip.

The Short List That Works

According to sports nutrition reviews, only a handful of supplements have strong evidence for building muscle: creatine monohydrate, protein powder, and to a lesser degree HMB and essential amino acids (ISSN exercise and sports nutrition review update).

SupplementEvidenceWhat it does
Creatine monohydrateStrongBoosts strength and muscle gain
Protein powderStrongHelps you hit daily protein easily
HMBModerateMostly helps beginners and older adults
CaffeineStrong (performance)Improves training output, not muscle directly

Creatine: The Top Pick

Creatine monohydrate is the most studied and most effective muscle supplement. Reviews confirm it augments the muscle-growth response to resistance training, and it is well tolerated at 3 to 5 g per day (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2021). No loading phase is required.

Protein Powder: A Convenience, Not Magic

Protein powder does not build muscle by itself. It just makes hitting your daily protein target easier. Total protein of 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg is what matters, whether it comes from food or a shake (Morton et al., 2018, British Journal of Sports Medicine).

What to Skip

Many popular products have little or no evidence for muscle growth. Reviews found that chromium, vanadyl sulfate, boron, and DHEA do not meaningfully increase lean mass (Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition). Vitamin D supplementation also showed no effect on muscle gain in people who are not deficient (Journal of Human Kinetics, 2024).

The Honest Bottom Line

Supplements are the small stuff. Proteins remain the most efficient supplement for muscle mass, with creatine as the best add-on (Journal of Human Kinetics, 2024). Everything else is a rounding error compared to training and total protein.

Key Takeaways

  • Creatine monohydrate (3 to 5 g/day) is the top muscle supplement.
  • Protein powder helps you hit your daily protein, nothing more.
  • HMB and caffeine have narrower, situational benefits.
  • Most other muscle supplements are not worth the money.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best supplement for muscle growth?

Creatine monohydrate. It has the strongest evidence and is safe at 3 to 5 g per day.

Do I need protein powder to build muscle?

No. It is a convenient way to hit your protein target, but food works just as well.

Are mass gainers or test boosters worth it?

Usually not. Most have little evidence for muscle growth compared to creatine and adequate protein.

Does vitamin D help build muscle?

Not in people who are not deficient. A meta-analysis found no effect on muscle gain or strength.
