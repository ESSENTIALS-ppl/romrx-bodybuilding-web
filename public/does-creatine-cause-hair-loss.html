Does Creatine Cause Hair Loss?

This is one of the most common fears about creatine. The worry started from a single old study. But newer research measured hair directly. Here is what it actually found.

The Short Answer: The Evidence Says No

The current evidence does not support a real link between creatine and hair loss. The myth traces back to one 2009 study on hormone levels, not on hair itself (The Strength Equation research update).

Where the Myth Came From

In 2009, a study in rugby players found a rise in DHT, a hormone linked to hereditary hair loss. But that study never measured hair. It only measured the hormone. That single result launched years of worry (Elithair review of the evidence). The finding was never clearly repeated in follow-up work.

What the Newest Study Found

In 2025, researchers ran the first trial that measured hair directly. Over 12 weeks, they tracked DHT, testosterone, and hair growth in creatine users versus placebo. The result: no significant change in DHT and no change in hair density or hair growth from creatine (Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2025).

The Evidence at a Glance

StudyWhat it measuredResult
2009 rugby studyDHT hormone only (no hair)DHT rose; hair never checked
2021 to 2025 follow-upsHormones and hairNo meaningful effect on hair
2025 controlled trial (12 weeks)DHT plus hair growth directlyNo change in DHT or hair

Multiple trials between 2021 and 2025 failed to replicate any real effect on hair (The Strength Equation research update).

Should You Worry?

For people with no genetic tendency toward male-pattern baldness, current evidence considers creatine safe for hair (Elithair review). If you already have a strong family history of hair loss, talk to your doctor. But the data do not show creatine as the cause.

Key Takeaways

  • Current evidence shows no link between creatine and hair loss.
  • The myth came from one 2009 study that measured a hormone, not hair.
  • A 2025 trial measured hair directly and found no change.
  • Creatine remains one of the safest, most studied supplements.
