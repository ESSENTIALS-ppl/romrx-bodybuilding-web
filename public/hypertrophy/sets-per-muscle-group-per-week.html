Hypertrophy Science

How Many Sets Per Muscle Group Per Week for Hypertrophy?

If you want to build muscle, weekly sets matter more than almost anything else. This is your total number of hard, challenging sets for one muscle across a full week. Get this number right and growth follows. This guide gives you the science-backed range and how to use it.

What the research says

More sets usually means more muscle, up to a point. A large review by Brad Schoenfeld and colleagues found that muscle growth rises as weekly sets rise. Groups doing 10 or more sets per week grew more than groups doing fewer (Schoenfeld et al., 2017, Journal of Sports Sciences).

Each extra weekly set was linked to about a 0.37% gain in muscle size in the pooled data, and 9 or more sets beat fewer than 9 (House of Hypertrophy summary of the Schoenfeld data).

The dose-response is not endless, though. More sets help until the body can no longer recover from them, and then extra volume just adds fatigue (Scinergy review of the Schoenfeld meta-analyses).

The practical range

Training levelWeekly sets per muscleNotes
Beginner8–12Grows well on lower volume; focus on form first
Intermediate12–18The sweet spot for most lifters
Advanced18–22+Only if recovery, sleep, and nutrition are dialed in

Start at the low end of your range. Add 1–2 sets per muscle every couple of weeks only if you are still recovering and progressing.

Why frequency changes the math

You do not need to cram all your sets into one day. Splitting weekly sets across two or more sessions lets each set be higher quality. Training a muscle at least twice per week beats once per week when total sets are equal (Verro Training review of frequency research).

So 16 weekly sets for back might become 8 sets on Monday and 8 on Thursday.

The ROMRx difference: quality sets, not just more sets

A "set" only counts if it drives the muscle through a deep, loaded stretch. Stretch-mediated hypertrophy research shows that training with a full range of motion, especially the lengthened position, produces equal or greater growth than partial-range work (The Strength Equation on lengthened partials).

Practical rule: count a set only if it hits full ROM under load. Ten deep, controlled sets beat sixteen shallow ones. This is how you get more growth from fewer, better sets.

Frequently asked questions

Is 10 sets per week enough to grow?

Yes. For most lifters, 10–12 hard weekly sets per muscle drives solid growth, especially for beginners and intermediates (Schoenfeld et al., 2017).

Can too many sets stop muscle growth?

Yes. Past your recovery limit, extra sets add fatigue without extra muscle (Scinergy review).

Should I split my sets across the week?

Yes. Training a muscle two or more times per week beats once per week when total volume is equal (Verro Training).
